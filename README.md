PROJECT NIFTY — NSE IPO Case Study

Overview

Project NIFTY is an investment-banking-style academic IPO case study of National Stock Exchange of India Limited (NSE).

The objective is to build a complete IPO analysis similar in structure to the work prepared by an investment bank for a prospective listing. The project combines company research, industry analysis, financial modelling, strategic analysis, valuation, IPO structuring, a simplified DRHP, book-building simulation, listing simulation and an investment recommendation.

Important: This is an academic / illustrative case study and is not investment advice, an offer to buy or sell securities, or an actual NSE IPO forecast.

Project Objectives

The case study aims to answer five core questions:

What is NSE and how does its business work?

How financially strong and sustainable is NSE?

What is NSE worth using multiple valuation approaches?

How should an NSE IPO be structured and priced?

Would an investor subscribe at the recommended IPO price?

Key Takeaways

NSE is positioned as India's dominant market infrastructure institution, with businesses spanning trading, clearing and settlement, indices, market data, technology, international operations and investor education.

The case study identifies NSE's major competitive advantage as its liquidity network effect: high liquidity attracts more traders and issuers, which further strengthens liquidity.

The company profile also highlights the NIFTY franchise, NSE Clearing, the growing investor base and NSE's dominant position relative to BSE.

The positive case rests on:

NSE's dominant market position.

Strong liquidity network effects.

High operating profitability.

Final View

At approximately ₹1,249/share, the case study considers NSE attractively positioned relative to its ₹1,419.80/share blended fair value, providing a valuation cushion.

The recommendation therefore remains:

SUBSCRIBE at ₹1,212–₹1,287, with ₹1,249 as the preferred modelling midpoint.

At significantly higher valuations, the margin of safety reduces and the risk-reward becomes less attractive.

Overall Project Investment Case

The complete case study presents NSE as a:

Dominant, highly profitable, capital-light financial-market-infrastructure franchise with strong network effects and balance-sheet strength, but with meaningful regulatory and governance-related risks.

The central investment debate is therefore not whether NSE is a high-quality business, but how much investors should pay for that quality.

The valuation work suggests that disciplined pricing is essential.

Source & Methodology Note

The project materials were prepared as an academic exercise using the project's supplied company profile, financial analysis, business analysis, valuation and IPO-structure materials.

The Phase 1 material states that figures were sourced from public disclosures, NSE's DRHP filed in June 2026 and media reporting. The Phase 4 material similarly identifies NSE's DRHP, corporate disclosures and public reporting as its source base.

For the final version of the project, statutory / regulatory information should always be checked against the latest NSE and SEBI filings before external publication or presentation.
